Teachers always said I had potential, but that I didn't use it to do well in my studies. I heard that a lot growing up.

I finally found out why, and it was all about my interest.

As this is my first blog, let me introduce myself. I'm Allen, an international student living in Canada. I came from India, and it's been almost a year and a half here.

I was a hardcore outdoor person into sports. I spent countless hours playing cricket and football in heavy sun and rain, because I loved it. With studies, I got decent marks even if I only studied in the last couple of days, but that wasn't enough to satisfy the potential me in any way.

I took computer science in higher secondary and also in college, and I still don't know why I made that decision. I never had any interest in computers. I still regret that I didn't find my real interest early and pursue what I love. This doesn't mean I don't love computers or software development now. It's just me overthinking how I ended up loving them.

Like I said, I had no interest back then, and while I was in college I decided not to build a career in software development. So when my friends were looking for jobs, I started looking for my interest.

After a while, I realised that I love movies, so I thought, why not try something in that field?? I didn't want to act, but I thought of doing something like editing. Somehow I wanted to see my name in the end credits.

Okay, now I had some plan. I started learning video editing through tutorials and experimenting on my own. Through a friend, I got the chance to be a promotional video editor for a Malayalam movie. When I saw my name in the end credits, I was happy and also proud. It was a small thing for others, but for me it meant I actually did something I wanted to do.

But then things turned upside down. I realised the financial issues and job insecurity this field had. I knew I would struggle in the beginning and I thought of facing it, but I couldn't hold on much longer thinking about my family. I come from a middle-class family, so I couldn't take that much risk.

So after spending 6 months or so, I started thinking about getting a job again. I started brainstorming, not about ideas, but about going back to the field, the only field I had a degree in.

I started learning programming through tutorials, and this time I actually spent time figuring out the things I failed to understand in higher secondary and college. Somehow I started finding the confidence that I could also do this. That led me to do a 6-month web development course. I was spending time on it outside of classes too, because I had already started liking what I was doing. For the first time, studying didn't feel forced. Again, it was all about interest.

After the course, I thought of studying abroad as well. My friends were also going abroad for higher studies, so I just went with the same idea. It was never in my dreams or anything, it was just going with the flow. But this time I was going to study something I actually had an interest in.

That's how I ended up in the software development program at Southern Alberta Institute of Technology. I think I'm doing better in my studies now, not in marks, but in class. I answer questions that no one else does, because I spend my own time learning and watching content on software development.

So I finally found something I love. But now it's changing in front of me.

Software development is like an ocean, and you can't master everything without working in the industry, right? So it's tough and makes me anxious most of the time. And now that we have AI, most or all of the code in whatever I'm working on is written by AI. I'm using Claude Code to write code. It's even making me dumb sometimes. I'm scared as well.

This is an entirely new era of software development, and you've got to adapt to it, or others will. Even if you say AI writes bad code, you can't think of writing all of that by yourself anymore. That's impossible. Writing code has never been this easy.

So where does that leave someone like me, who just found his interest in this field? I still love software development. But I think I should start looking for a new interest, maybe in areas of development where human judgement is still needed. Or maybe in some other field or job, if software development is not there in the future. I hope that doesn't happen, but I don't know yet.

What I do know is that I will perform well if I find it, because for me it is all about interest.
